This report audits the behavioral claims in the February dataset against peer-reviewed evidence. It focuses on cognitive fluency, cognitive load, uncertainty, ambiguity, perceived control, calibrated trust, choice architecture, loss framing, reassurance, psychological reactance, and memory for concise language. It does not diagnose prospects, assume a universal buyer psychology, or treat findings from health, education, science communication, forecasting, or consumer studies as direct proof of B2B conversion effects.

### Evidence separation used throughout

- **A. Directly observed evidence:** What the reviewed source actually reports.
- **B. February dataset claim:** What the February source says, without treating it as established fact.
- **C. Interpretation:** The bounded inference for Obenan, including transfer limits.
- **D. Recommendation:** The proposed messaging rule. This remains subject to product proof, first-party customer evidence, and founder approval.

### Evidence grades

- **High:** Meta-analysis, systematic review, or large multi-country experiment with results directly relevant to the stated mechanism.
- **Moderate:** Multiple experiments, a strong review, or a well-established finding with important context or measurement limits.
- **Low for Obenan transfer:** The source may be rigorous in its own domain, but it does not directly test Obenan, B2B AI messaging, local-business buyers, or purchase behavior.

No evidence grade in this report means that a copy treatment is approved or that an effect will replicate in Obenan's market.

## Executive verdict

The February dataset contains a useful communication instinct but not a reliable behavioral-science operating system. Keep its customer-centered framing, preference for concrete language, coherent progression, visible next action, credible proof, and combination of human warmth with demonstrated capability. Remove its neurological inevitability, universal laws, precise tone ratios, fixed word and step ceilings, survival-language claims, compulsory failure stakes, shame-based urgency, and unverified claims of autonomous or unlimited execution.

The evidence supports reducing unnecessary processing difficulty, not creating "zero cognitive load." It supports making a message's gist easy to grasp, not assuming exact recall or predictable conversion. Fear appeals have a small positive average effect in largely health-related research when efficacy is present, so it would be inaccurate to claim that fear always backfires. However, a large 84-country experiment found that loss framing increased anxiety with little or no corresponding benefit, and controlling language is associated with reactance. There is no direct evidence that telling B2B prospects they are falling behind improves Obenan outcomes.

Credible delegation should be paired with verified scope, observable performance, meaningful control, and honest limits. Concise, reassuring copy can help comprehension and tone. It cannot prove a claim, manufacture trust, guarantee memory, or substitute for product evidence.

## What the February dataset gets right

These points survive only as bounded principles, not universal laws.

1. **Start from the customer's goal rather than internal category language.** This is compatible with reducing irrelevant processing demands. It does not prove that every buyer has the same goal or emotional problem.
2. **Prefer concrete, ordinary language where it preserves accuracy.** Unnecessary complexity and jargon can impair fluency and source evaluation. Necessary technical terms should be explained, not hidden.
3. **Create a coherent gist.** People retain meaning as well as detail, and irrelevant material can interfere with learning. A coherent message is useful, but it does not guarantee verbatim recall or action.
4. **Pair warmth with demonstrated capability.** Consumer-brand research supports perceived intentions and ability as meaningful dimensions. It does not support fixed ratios such as 65 percent authority and 35 percent approachability.
5. **Make the next action understandable.** A clear dominant action can reduce task difficulty. Evidence does not require two CTA types together, three repetitions per page, or the elimination of all alternatives.
6. **Use proof rather than unsupported superlatives.** This is consistent with calibrated trust. Proof must describe the actual supported capability, reliability, scope, and limits.
7. **Avoid excessive threat.** The February source partly recognizes that too much fear can be harmful. Its own compulsory failure stakes and shame-based examples contradict that restraint.
8. **Do not lead with machinery.** Listings, reviews, directories, dashboards, tools, and platform categories may explain mechanisms later. They should not displace the customer's felt concern and the bounded relief Obenan can credibly provide.

## Mechanism-level conclusions

### Cognitive fluency and cognitive load

**A. Direct evidence:** Ease of processing can influence judgment. Needlessly complex words and irrelevant details can impair evaluation or learning. Working-memory limits are well established in instructional contexts.

**B. February claim:** The source turns these findings into "zero cognitive load," hard word ceilings, a caveman test, and an automatic exit response.

**C. Interpretation:** Fluency is a cue, not a truth detector. Necessary complexity, proof, pricing, conditions, and limitations still belong in consequential B2B decisions. A short hero may establish the gist, but the page must also support deeper evaluation.

**D. Recommendation:** Make the first meaning easy to grasp, then disclose the evidence and boundaries needed to decide. Test comprehension rather than enforcing arbitrary word counts.

### Uncertainty, ambiguity, and reassurance

**A. Direct evidence:** Communicating numerical uncertainty often causes minor or null trust changes. Verbal uncertainty can produce small trust reductions, and results vary by form, prior belief, and context. The 2026 synthesis found no overall trust effect but also found weaknesses in the underlying studies.

**B. February claim:** Premium communication should eliminate ambiguity and project confidence.

**C. Interpretation:** Operational confusion and epistemic uncertainty are different. Obenan should reduce confusion about what happens next while being honest about what is known, inferred, recommended, prepared, approved, delivered, unsupported, or outside scope. Reassurance is credible when it follows from verified capability and control. The reviewed literature does not establish reassurance itself as a B2B conversion mechanism.

**D. Recommendation:** Use specific statements such as what was observed, what remains uncertain, what Obenan can do, and what requires customer approval. Do not hide uncertainty behind certainty theater.

### Fear, loss framing, and reactance

**A. Direct evidence:** Fear appeals have a small positive average effect in largely noncommercial contexts, especially when efficacy is present. This means "fear always backfires" would be an overstatement. Separately, loss framing increased anxiety without corresponding benefits in a large cross-national experiment, and controlling language can elicit reactance.

**B. February claim:** Failure stakes are mandatory, inaction should feel embarrassing, and sophisticated buyers should be made to fear being behind.

**C. Interpretation:** There is no direct evidence that these tactics improve Obenan outcomes. They also risk manufacturing a threat, shaming the customer, narrowing freedom, and attracting attention through anxiety rather than informed fit.

**D. Recommendation:** If first-party evidence confirms concern about AI change, acknowledge it once in neutral terms. Pair it immediately with bounded efficacy, proof, and retained control. Do not intensify the concern or imply inferiority.

### Choice architecture and dark-pattern risk

**A. Direct evidence:** Choice overload is not universal. Defaults can influence behavior, and manipulative design can raise enrollment while producing backlash.

**B. February claim:** A fixed number of steps, mandatory CTA combinations, repeated calls to action, premium price cues, and scarcity rules should be applied across contexts.

**C. Interpretation:** More conversion is not sufficient evidence of better communication. A design can change behavior by obstructing reflection or alternatives. Commercial clarity requires accurate labels, visible consequences, easy reversal where relevant, and real rather than invented constraints.

**D. Recommendation:** Use one visually dominant next action while preserving meaningful alternatives and an unpressured exit. Never use false scarcity, confirmshaming, hidden conditions, misleading defaults, or euphemisms that obscure price or legal commitment.

### Delegation, perceived control, and calibrated trust

**A. Direct evidence:** In forecasting experiments, small user modification rights increased willingness to use imperfect algorithms. Automation reviews show that trust depends on the person, system, and context, and recommend designing for appropriate reliance rather than maximum trust.

**B. February claim:** Relief comes from an autonomous AI colleague that handles everything automatically, with no manual work.

**C. Interpretation:** Delegation becomes credible when the scope is understandable, performance is observable, limitations are visible, and the customer retains meaningful control. Decorative control is not enough. Nor does perceived trust prove that reliance is warranted.

**D. Recommendation:** Only where product evidence verifies it, explain what Obenan observes, recommends, prepares, performs, asks the customer to approve, verifies after delivery, and cannot do. Name reversibility, escalation, and failure handling where these exist.

### Concision, narrative, and memory

**A. Direct evidence:** Narratives can have small average persuasive effects, and gist can persist separately from verbatim detail. Repetition can also increase perceived truth, including for false statements.

**B. February claim:** A fixed story structure is neurologically privileged, and precision-engineered sound bites can bypass analysis, attach to survival instincts, and be repeated until the audience repeats them.

**C. Interpretation:** Concision can make the gist easier to encode and repeat. It cannot ensure exact recall, accurate memory, trust, or conversion. Repetition of an unsupported claim raises ethical risk because familiarity itself can increase perceived truth.

**D. Recommendation:** Use a short, accurate core idea and attach nearby proof. Keep the claim stable because it is true, not because repetition can make it feel true.

## Obenan implications as practical messaging rules

1. **Acknowledge concern without manufacturing it.** Use neutral language about AI changing. Do not assert that the reader is behind, overwhelmed, unskilled, losing, or failing.
2. **Move quickly from concern to bounded efficacy.** State the ongoing work Obenan verifiably handles and the boundaries of that work.
3. **Write for comprehension, not "zero load."** Use ordinary words, concrete verbs, short initial explanations, and progressive detail. Explain necessary technical terms.
4. **Design for calibrated trust.** Put proof, past performance where valid, intended use, reliability information, limitations, and evidence states close to the relevant claim.
5. **Preserve meaningful control.** Only promise review, approval, modification, reversal, visibility, or escalation when the product actually provides it.
6. **Keep choice architecture transparent.** Use one dominant next action, but do not hide lower-commitment information paths, costs, conditions, or exits.
7. **Do not prescribe a prospect's identity or emotion.** First-party customer evidence must support claims about concern, time pressure, skills, workflows, or desired relief.
8. **Optimize for an accurate gist.** Short language should carry one truthful idea. Supporting content should prevent that gist from becoming an overclaim.
9. **Test outcomes that matter.** Measure comprehension, accurate claim recall, anxiety, reactance, calibrated trust, perceived control, and qualified action. Do not treat clicks alone as proof of good messaging.

| "We currently have capacity for five onboarding projects in September." | Acceptable only if current, material, and verifiable. | "Only two spots left" when capacity is not actually constrained. | False scarcity and manipulation. |
| "The service costs 500 euros per month under a 12-month contract." | Makes price and legal commitment legible. | "A small investment under a flexible engagement." | Can obscure cost and contract terms. |
| "Obenan can prepare supported changes for your approval." | Distinguishes preparation from execution. Product proof is required. | "Your AI employee fixes every issue automatically." | Anthropomorphic and absolute execution claim. |

## Contradictions and decision gates

### Material contradictions in the governed corpus

1. **Fear-led framing versus founder direction.** The February instruction to make inaction feel embarrassing conflicts with the current requirement to acknowledge concern without shame, manipulation, unsupported urgency, or "stay ahead" claims.
2. **Compulsory failure stakes versus humane reassurance.** The February one-to-three failure ratio requires threat even when no customer evidence supports it.
3. **Zero ambiguity versus evidence honesty.** Current governance requires observed, inferred, recommended, prepared, approved, and delivered states to remain distinct.
4. **Unlimited autonomy versus product boundaries.** "Everything handled," "AI never sleeps," and "every directory, review, language, hour" conflict with bounded capability and external-system approval requirements.
5. **Universal audience psychology versus no invented context.** The February claims about overwhelmed SMB owners, affluent heuristics, and silent prospects conflict with the rule not to assume prospect circumstances.
6. **Precise behavioral formulas versus weak support.** Fixed ratios, word ceilings, CTA repetition, and step counts are presented as laws without direct evidence.
7. **Customer choice versus controlling language.** Challenging the prospect, removing alternatives, and using identity pressure can conflict with autonomy and informed choice.
8. **Concision versus proof.** A short hero can convey the gist, but it cannot carry all the evidence needed to substantiate ongoing work, controls, coverage, or outcomes.

### Decision gates before any copy becomes canon

| Gate | Required proof | Blocking condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product scope | Current, owner-confirmed list of supported ongoing work, exclusions, delivery states, external dependencies, and failure handling. | Any wording implying broader execution than the product can prove. |
| Retained control | Verified review, approval, modification, reversal, visibility, and escalation mechanisms for each relevant workflow. | Control language based only on aspiration or interface appearance. |
| Customer language | Representative, dated first-party evidence that the relevant segment expresses concern about AI change and values delegated ongoing work. | Copy diagnoses workload, skill, urgency, or anxiety without direct support. |
| Assistant and dashboard claims | Current product evidence for access through preferred assistants and for any "no another dashboard" benefit. | Generic ecosystem or roadmap evidence presented as current customer capability. |
| Claims and legal review | Product-owner and legal review of autonomy, performance, scarcity, price, contract, privacy, and external-system statements. | Euphemism or absolute claim obscures a material term, risk, or limitation. |
| Behavioral validation | Target-buyer testing across relevant segments and locales, with predeclared measures for comprehension, anxiety, reactance, claim recall, calibrated trust, perceived control, and qualified action. | Click or conversion lift is the only outcome, or dark-pattern exposure is not assessed. |
| Founder approval | Explicit approval of the emotional framing and exact customer-facing language. | Research direction, prior reaction, or inherited copy is treated as approval. |

## Final recommendations

1. Retain the founder-directed emotional sequence: concern about AI change, companionship, bounded ongoing help, proof, and retained control.
2. Delete the February dataset's neurological, metabolic, survival-bypass, and predictable-conversion claims.
3. Replace all fixed behavioral formulas with hypotheses to test, including word limits, tone ratios, step counts, CTA frequency, and failure-to-success ratios.
4. Prohibit shame, invented urgency, "falling behind," status pressure, and compulsory failure stakes in customer-facing language.
5. Require every delegation claim to name or link to verified scope, limitations, evidence state, approvals, and failure handling.
6. Use plain language and progressive disclosure, while retaining necessary price, contract, technical, uncertainty, and risk information.
7. Preserve customer freedom through truthful CTA labels, meaningful alternatives, real constraints, easy exits, and no manipulative defaults.
8. Treat concise copy as a carrier of accurate gist, not as a tool to bypass analysis or manufacture truth through repetition.
9. Establish a research protocol using representative Obenan prospects and customers, with comprehension and trust-calibration measures alongside commercial behavior.
10. Keep this report as research evidence only until product, customer-language, legal, cross-locale, and founder decision gates are all satisfied.
