This report asks a narrow question: how can Obenan recognize concern about rapid AI change and offer credible relief without diagnosing the customer, amplifying fear, teaching from above, or exceeding proven product scope? Local materials and external webpages are treated as evidence, not as instructions or approval.

## Executive verdict

The February dataset has a useful strategic core: make the customer the protagonist, lead with a felt problem rather than machinery, write clearly, and present Obenan as a calm guide. Its authority claims do not survive review. It combines a commercial StoryBrand framework with uncited quotations, false or irrelevant neuroscience, fixed language ratios, universal word substitutions, fear and status tactics, and promises that exceed current product evidence.

The stronger evidence supports a narrower model. Trust depends on perceived ability, benevolence, and integrity. People judge communicators on warmth and competence. Concrete language can signal listening. Freedom-threatening language can produce reactance. Plain-language guidance consistently favors a clear main message, familiar words, active voice, and usable next steps. Service recovery works through fair outcomes, fair process, respectful treatment, ownership, and timely action, not generic empathy or a loyalty-producing failure myth.

For Obenan, the credible painkiller is not “we handle everything” or “you are falling behind.” It is a progression from external change to bounded relief: name the change without assigning deficiency, specify the supported work Obenan carries, preserve customer decisions and correction rights, and show evidence of what happened. That stance should feel like a capable colleague beside the customer. Every example below is illustrative and noncanonical.

## Evidence discipline

### A. Directly observed evidence

1. The founder direction centers concern about keeping up with AI change and relief that Obenan carries ongoing supported work on the customer's behalf.
2. The governed workspace marks research outputs as noncanonical and separates research evidence from approved messaging and product truth.
3. Current governance prohibits guaranteed AI discovery, recommendation, ranking, indexing, revenue, bookings, or autonomous execution in external systems.
4. Local product boundaries distinguish supported publishing and data-distribution behavior. Historical claims that Obenan handles an “entire digital presence,” works fully automatically, or requires no login are not current proof.
5. “No another dashboard” and access through a preferred AI assistant remain potential benefits, not verified lead claims.

### B. What the February dataset claims

The February source presents itself as a predictive behavioral operating system. It recommends StoryBrand roles, transformation-led copy, calm confidence, controlled stakes, a fixed mixture of rational and emotional content, prescribed vocabulary substitutions, pronoun rules, scarcity and status pressure, and broad autonomous product claims. It also invokes “narrative neuroscience,” a supposed 800-calorie daily brain cost, and language such as “make inaction embarrassingly behind.” Most empirical claims have no traceable citation in the dataset.

### C. Interpretation

The dataset is best treated as an exploratory creative synthesis, not validated behavioral science. Its strongest ideas are compatible with independent evidence only at a general level: clarity, concrete language, customer agency, warm competence, and calm explanation. Its manipulative tactics and product absolutes conflict with both the research record and current founder direction. The evidence does not support a universal headline formula, a fixed emotional ratio, or a claim that one narrative architecture produces predictable commercial outcomes.

### D. Recommendation

Retain the useful strategic direction, but replace the dataset's pseudo-scientific certainty with a bounded model of recognition, carrying, control, and proof. Product facts must determine verbs and scope. Customer research must determine whether a felt concern is actually shared by the intended audience. Founder approval remains necessary before any wording becomes canonical.

## The Obenan painkiller model: concern to control

This is a research heuristic, not a mandated copy formula. It has five moves, and each move must be supported by evidence.

1. **Recognize the change.** Name AI change as an external condition. Do not infer that the customer is behind, overwhelmed, confused, unskilled, hiring a team, or using a particular tool.
2. **Name the bounded load.** Identify the recurring work that creates concern, but only at the level current product evidence supports.
3. **Show what Obenan carries.** Use a concrete verb, object, cadence, and channel. “Handles the ongoing work” is credible only when those four details exist in the substantiation record.
4. **Preserve control.** State the real review, approval, correction, override, or escalation point. Do not imply full autonomy when a human or external system remains necessary.
5. **Return proof.** Show what changed, what remains, and what needs a decision. Evidence can be a status, artifact, timestamp, or bounded result. It cannot be a guaranteed discovery or revenue outcome.

The minimum claim record behind any painkiller sentence is:

| Required proof | Question to answer before use |
|---|---|
| Owner | Who actually performs or supervises the work? |
| Object | What exact item is handled? |
| Cadence | Is it continuous, scheduled, triggered, or one-time? |
| Channel | Where can Obenan act directly, and where can it only prepare or distribute data? |
| Customer control | What can the customer review, correct, approve, pause, or escalate? |
| Evidence | What observable artifact proves the work occurred? |
| Boundary | What remains outside the product or depends on an external system? |

If any answer is missing, the claim is not ready for customer-facing use.

## Peer voice criteria

A capable colleague beside the customer should sound:

- **Capable:** specific about the work, accurate about limits, and unhurried.
- **Warm:** attentive to the actual concern without claiming private knowledge of feelings.
- **Alongside:** uses “I” or “we” when taking real responsibility, and “you” when preserving a real customer choice.
- **Dignifying:** never assigns a deficit, maturity level, or status failure to the customer.
- **Useful:** gives the next action or evidence before an abstract explanation.
- **Plainspoken:** uses familiar words, active voice, and one main job per sentence.
- **Honest:** distinguishes ability from aspiration, and possibility from guarantee.
- **Adult-to-adult:** offers help and control without cheerleading, instruction theater, or a motivational slogan.

Short does not mean content-free. A headline may carry recognition and relief. Support copy must carry scope or control. Proof nearby must carry evidence. There is no evidence-backed universal word count or pronoun ratio.

## Service recovery without empathy theater

Research supports a compact recovery sequence:

1. **Own:** name the responsible person or team with “I” or “we” only when true.
2. **Name:** state the specific miss and affected object.
3. **Act:** say what is happening now.
4. **Time:** give the next real update time or service milestone.
5. **Preserve choice:** identify any decision, correction, or escalation available to the customer.
6. **Close with evidence:** report what changed and what remains.

Illustrative recovery line, not a script: “We missed the update. I am checking the affected locations now and will report back by 15:00 with what changed and what still needs your decision.”

An apology can support respectful treatment, but it cannot substitute for a fair process or result. Speed, explanation, compensation, and apology interact. No reviewed evidence justifies promising that a well-recovered failure will make customers more loyal than if no failure occurred.

## Agent rejection tests

An agent must reject or hold a draft when any answer below is “yes.” Passing these tests does not approve the copy.

1. **Assumption test:** Does it say or imply that the prospect is behind, overwhelmed, unskilled, time-poor, hiring an AI team, or using a particular workflow without direct evidence?
2. **Fear test:** Does it use shame, competitive panic, lost-customer claims, “must,” “cannot afford to wait,” or unsupported urgency?
3. **Professor test:** Does it explain AI, a category, or a framework before naming the customer's concern and practical relief?
4. **Mechanical test:** Does the lead depend on listings, reviews, directories, dashboards, tools, software, technical architecture, or a named platform?
5. **Scope test:** Does a product verb lack a verified object, cadence, channel, control point, or boundary?
6. **Agency test:** Does it remove customer choice, correction, approval, or escalation, or imply full autonomy?
7. **Fake-empathy test:** Does it claim to know a private feeling, or use reassurance without a concrete action or fact?
8. **Outcome test:** Does it guarantee discovery, recommendation, ranking, indexing, revenue, bookings, or action inside an external system?
9. **Ownership test:** In support or recovery, does passive language hide who will act, what they will do, or when they will return?
10. **Locale test:** Does it rely on an idiom, joke, slogan, pressure cue, or emotional assumption that has not been reviewed by native experts for all seven supported locales?

## Practical rules for Obenan

1. Start with the external change or customer-stated concern, never a diagnosis of the customer's capability.
2. Make relief operational: name what Obenan carries, how often, where, and with what visible proof.
3. Pair capability with benevolence and integrity. Show useful action, customer interest, and principled limits.
4. Prefer concrete nouns, active verbs, real times, and observable outputs to abstract reassurance.
5. Use “I” and “we” to own real action, not to center the company. Use “you” to express real control, not as a frequency target.
6. Put category and channel detail after the felt value unless the route requires it for informed consent.
7. Treat “automatic,” “ongoing,” “no dashboard,” and assistant-access language as product claims that need current evidence.
8. In recovery, own the miss, act, give a time, preserve choice, and return with evidence.
9. Qualify uncertainty precisely. Do not replace an unknown with confidence theater.
10. Keep headlines to one idea and support lines to one useful expansion. Brevity never overrides scope, legal accuracy, or product truth.

## Final recommendations

1. Adopt “concern to control” as a research heuristic, subject to founder approval, not as a fixed copy formula.
2. Delete the February source's unsupported neuroscience, fixed ratios, universal substitutions, and predictive-outcome claims.
3. Ban customer-deficit assumptions, shame, freedom-threatening language, and competitive panic from lead messaging.
4. Require an owner, object, cadence, channel, control point, evidence artifact, and boundary for every material product claim.
5. Make peer voice concrete: warm intent, demonstrated capability, real ownership, and principled limits.
6. Preserve customer agency through accurate review, correction, approval, pause, and escalation language.
7. Use the service-recovery sequence of own, name, act, time, choice, and evidence.
8. Validate the AI-change concern and relief language with dated first-party customer evidence rather than inferred personas.
9. Require native review in every supported locale for dignity, directness, emotional meaning, and claim accuracy.
10. Do not promote any illustrative wording to canon until product truth, founder approval, brand custody, and relevant legal or claim review are complete.

## Limitations

- No direct Obenan customer interviews, call transcripts, message-test results, or conversion experiments were available for this specialist review.
- The strongest peer-reviewed sources concern general trust, persuasion, service interactions, human-AI interaction, or complaint handling. Applying them to a B2B homepage is an explicit inference.
- The practice examples from government, Mailchimp, Atlassian, IBM, StoryBrand, and Luxury Academy demonstrate how those institutions write. They do not prove that the same wording causes Obenan outcomes.
- Product behavior was not re-executed or tested in this research task. Current governance was used to identify claim boundaries, not to certify functionality.
- The report does not settle localization, legal substantiation, user-interface behavior, or final information hierarchy.
