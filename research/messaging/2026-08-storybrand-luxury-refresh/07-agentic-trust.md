# Agentic trust, bounded delegation, and interface choice

## Metadata and scope

- **Status:** Research evidence only. Not approved copy, product truth, brand canon, or release authority.
- **Specialist role:** Agentic Trust Researcher.
- **Research cutoff:** 2026-08-19.
- **External retrieval date:** 2026-08-19 for every web source in this report.
- **Question:** How can Obenan describe work performed on a customer's behalf without implying unsupported autonomy, weak oversight, or guaranteed outcomes?
- **Included:** Trust in automation, delegation, human oversight, transparency, controllability, calibrated reliance, action security, recovery, reduced dashboard dependence, and preferred-assistant access.
- **Excluded:** Verification of the production application, legal compliance conclusions, security certification, approved copy, and proof of commercial outcomes.
- **Internal proof boundary:** The governed design workspace is a research and design repository, not the production application or release system [L03-L06]. External research cannot prove an Obenan capability. Every internal capability claim below remains gated on current authoritative product evidence.

### Evidence categories used

- **A. Directly observed:** What the cited local files or external source actually state or report.
- **B. February dataset claim:** What the preserved February messaging dataset says, whether or not it is true [L15-L16].
- **C. Interpretation:** A bounded conclusion drawn from A or B, with limitations retained.
- **D. Recommendation:** A proposed messaging or product-evidence rule. It is not a finding about current Obenan implementation.

## Executive verdict

The defensible trust objective is calibrated reliance, not maximum trust. Research repeatedly shows that people can both over-rely on automation and reject useful automation, and that explanations alone do not reliably correct either problem [E01-E05, E08-E11]. For agentic B2B messaging, "works on your behalf" should therefore mean bounded delegation with proof: the customer knows the supported scope, grants defined authority, can intervene at meaningful points, can see what happened, and has a recovery path.

The February dataset is directionally right to sell relief rather than machinery, position the customer as the hero, and treat clarity and honest communication as trust builders [L15]. It becomes unsafe when it turns that relief into absolute autonomy: "handles everything," "fixes them autonomously," "never sleeps," "no login required," or universal coverage. Those statements collapse monitoring, analysis, recommendation, preparation, approval, execution, delivery, and verification into one unproved promise.

The credible direction is active but bounded: Obenan carries supported ongoing work within customer-set boundaries, asks for judgment where required, and proves material changes. "No new dashboard to manage day to day" and access through a preferred assistant may be useful benefits, but only after current workflow, security, reliability, and user evidence supports the exact wording. This workspace does not provide that product proof.

## What the February dataset gets right

The following are useful directions, not validated conversion laws:

1. **Lead with the customer's outcome and relief.** The dataset tries to make the customer the hero and Obenan the guide rather than opening with product machinery [L15]. That aligns with Seven's current direction, provided the relief stays within verified boundaries.
2. **Prefer clarity to category language.** Short, concrete language can reduce the effort needed to understand an offer. The evidence supports disclosing capabilities, limits, and state clearly, not hiding them behind technical terminology [E07, E21-E24].
3. **Treat trust as earned through conduct.** The dataset's emphasis on consistent delivery, proactive communication, and not hiding bad news is compatible with research on appropriate reliance and with formal risk guidance [E01-E03, E21-E24].
4. **Avoid manufactured scarcity.** The dataset rejects false urgency in some sections [L15]. That restraint should govern the whole narrative, including AI-change anxiety.
5. **Make the service feel active.** Customers should not hear that Obenan merely produces advice. The safe alternative is to specify which work is monitored, prepared, approved, acted on, delivered, and verified rather than using a universal autonomy label.

## What must be corrected, removed, qualified, or updated

### Remove from customer-facing use unless exact current proof exists

The February source contains or endorses claims equivalent to "actively manages ... without you touching a button," "every location, every directory, every review, every language, every hour handled," "software manages itself," "fixes them autonomously," "handles everything automatically," "no login required," and an AI colleague that "never sleeps" [L15]. These should not be softened cosmetically. They should be removed until each implied capability and outcome is proven.

Why:

- They erase the difference between acquiring information, analysing it, selecting a decision, and implementing an action [E04].
- They encourage reliance without communicating reliability, state, limits, or exceptions [E01-E05].
- Anthropomorphic colleague language can raise expectations beyond actual conversational-agent capability [E13, E16].
- Vague, high-level delegation can weaken accountability and permit undesired conduct; this has been demonstrated in bounded ethics experiments, though not in Obenan's domain [E19].
- "No login" and universal assistant access imply authentication, authorization, data-handling, and recovery properties that must be proven, not inferred from an interface concept [E24-E27].

### Qualify or retire weak behavioral claims

The February dataset presents broad neuroscience and behavioral-psychology statements, fixed emotional/rational ratios, "survival instinct" mechanisms, and predictable response claims without traceable primary support [L15]. This report found no basis for using those statements as causal conversion proof. They should be retired or treated as unvalidated creative hypotheses. Correlation, a design heuristic, and a laboratory effect are not interchangeable with conversion causation.

### Update the trust premise

"Absolute confidence" is the wrong target. A trustworthy service should make appropriate reliance possible by exposing the evidence needed to accept, correct, stop, or escalate work. Transparency is necessary but insufficient: explanations have sometimes increased acceptance even when automation was wrong, and simpler explanations have not consistently improved joint performance [E05, E09-E11]. The message should promise inspectability and bounded control only where the product supplies them.

## Evidence-backed trust model for agentic B2B messaging

### Model: bounded delegation with proof

This is a recommendation synthesized from the evidence, not an externally standardized taxonomy and not a statement of current Obenan implementation.

| Element | Customer question | Minimum product reality before messaging it | Evidence basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Scope** | What exactly are you carrying for me? | Named supported jobs, objects, sources, systems, cadence, exclusions, and known limits | Function and autonomy level must be separated [E04]; agent powers should be bounded by use case, tools, and data [E24]. |
| **2. Authority** | What may you do, for whom, and for how long? | Explicit grant, role and tenant binding, least privilege, action limits, expiry or revocation, and no privilege elevation | Agent permissions should be scoped; processes acting for users should have only necessary privileges [E24-E27]. |
| **3. Visibility** | What is happening and what do you know? | Current state, provenance, uncertainty, coverage, material reasons, failures, and changed assumptions | Users need capabilities, limits, state, and intelligible output [E07, E21-E24]. |
| **4. Meaningful control** | When can I correct, approve, stop, or reverse it? | Dismissal, correction, approval, override, stop, and revocation at risk-appropriate checkpoints | Control must be usable in time by a competent, authorized person, not merely nominal [E12, E20, E23-E26]. |
| **5. Verification** | How do I know the intended result occurred? | Provider or system receipt plus authoritative read-back or downstream postcondition; partial and failure states remain visible | A plausible plan or explanation is not an outcome [E10, E11, E17, E22]. |
| **6. Accountability and recovery** | Who owns exceptions and what happens after failure? | Named owner, audit trail, escalation, retry or rollback design, incident path, and tested recovery | Continuous monitoring, incident response, and accountable oversight are required for credible delegation [E21-E24]. |
| **7. Interface fit** | Can I use this without losing essential control? | The chosen interface preserves status, approvals, evidence, error handling, history, privacy, and accessible fallback | Lower-friction interaction can help simple tasks, but reduced feedback can undermine confidence for complex delegation [E14-E18]. |

### Messaging sequence

1. Name the felt concern without fear amplification: AI keeps changing.
2. Offer bounded relief: Obenan carries supported ongoing work.
3. Preserve agency: the customer sets boundaries and supplies judgment where it matters.
4. State proof: Obenan shows what changed, what did not, and what still needs attention.

This sequence keeps the customer accompanied by a capable peer. It does not require category machinery, a professor-like lesson, or a claim of unrestricted autonomy.

## Claim ladder

The ladder prevents a lower state from being presented as a higher one. It is a recommended evidence vocabulary, not proof that Obenan currently supports every state.

| State | Meaning | Minimum evidence | Must not be presented as |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Observed** | A source, system, or event was directly inspected at a stated time. | Timestamp, source identity, captured result, and coverage limits. | Inferred cause, recommendation, or completed action. |
| **Inferred** | A conclusion is drawn from observations. | Linked observations, reasoning, confidence, and credible alternatives. | Direct observation or certainty. |
| **Recommended** | A next step is proposed. | Rationale, intended outcome, risks, and scope. | Decision, approval, or execution. |
| **Prepared** | A versioned draft or action artifact exists. | Exact artifact, source provenance, validation results, and explicit no-execution state. | Approval, delivery, or external change. |
| **Approved** | An authorized person approved the exact version and scope. | Approver authority, artifact version, scope, time, and conditions. | Execution or delivery. |
| **Delivered** | The exact artifact or action reached the intended target. | Target identity, version, timestamp, and provider or system receipt. | Correct downstream effect. |
| **Verified** | An authoritative read-back or postcondition confirms the intended result. | Independent check against success criteria, including scope and time. | Permanent correctness or guaranteed business outcome. |

**Cross-cutting states:** failed, partial, expired, superseded, revoked, rolled back, and unknown. These states must remain visible. No rung automatically implies the next. "Delivered" is not "verified," and "verified" does not guarantee discovery, ranking, revenue, bookings, or future persistence.

## Minimum proof for high-risk capability verbs

| Verb | Minimum proof before external use | Safer fallback when proof is incomplete |
|---|---|---|
| **Monitors** | Named object and source; trigger or cadence; coverage and blind spots; last successful run; current health; threshold; alert and escalation path; retained observations over time. A single run is not ongoing monitoring. | "Checks" for a proven point-in-time inspection, or "can review" for an available manual capability. |
| **Prepares** | Versioned artifact exists; inputs and provenance are recorded; policy, format, and compatibility checks pass; the artifact is visibly a draft; no external side effect occurred. | "Drafts" or "proposes." |
| **Decides** | Exact delegated decision right; bounded policy and exclusions; sufficient inputs; uncertainty handling; exception and escalation logic; accountable owner; timestamped decision log. Without this, the system recommends. | "Recommends" or "flags a choice." |
| **Acts** | Authenticated scoped identity; least-privilege permission; exact authorization context; input validation; sensitive-action confirmation where required; execution receipt; idempotency or replay protection; stop and recovery path; audit record. | "Can carry out approved steps" if approval and execution are both proven. |
| **Fixes** | Baseline defect; applied change; independent postcondition confirming resolution; negative, compatibility, and regression checks; rollback path; residual limitations. An attempted change is not a fix. | "Helps resolve," "prepares a correction," or "attempts the approved change," matched to the actual state. |
| **Handles** | A defined end-to-end scope; proven coverage of expected cases; exception and escalation ownership; authority boundaries; delivery receipts; verification; ongoing reliability evidence. Never append "everything." | "Handles the supported ongoing work" only if that exact scope is documented and proven. |
| **Delivers** | Exact target, artifact or action version, timestamp, and acknowledgment or provider receipt. Preparation and approval do not prove delivery. | "Prepares" or "is ready for approval." |
| **Works on your behalf** | The relevant scope, authority, control, receipt, verification, and recovery requirements above are met for every included action class. | "Works with you on" or "keeps the supported work moving" if execution authority is narrower. |

## Retaining human control without sounding passive

These are research examples, not approved Obenan copy. Every capability verb remains subject to the proof table.

### Safer examples

- "You set the boundaries. Obenan keeps the supported work moving and shows you what changed."
- "Obenan handles the ongoing work you authorize and brings you the moments that need your judgment."
- "The routine work keeps moving. You step in when your judgment matters."
- "Stay in control without doing every step."
- "Let Obenan carry the work, with clear boundaries and a record of what changed."
- "Obenan monitors the supported work, prepares the next step, and acts where you have authorized it."
- "Use Obenan in an assistant you already use."
- "No new dashboard to manage day to day."

The last three examples are safe only if every named capability, supported access route, and normal workflow is currently verified.

### Unsafe examples

- "Our AI handles everything automatically."
- "An AI colleague that never sleeps."
- "No login required."
- "It decides and fixes your digital presence for you."
- "Set it and forget it."
- "Every location. Every channel. Every hour. Handled."
- "Works in any assistant."
- "No dashboard, no oversight, no work."
- "Guaranteed discovery while you sleep."

The unsafe examples are absolute, anthropomorphic, unverifiable, or outcome-guaranteeing. They also obscure the customer's authority and the system's failure states.

## Boundaries for reduced dashboard dependence

### What the evidence supports

Task switching and interruptions can leave attention residue or increase stress and effort [E14-E15]. This supports an **interpretation** that fewer required context switches may reduce friction. It does not prove that an assistant interface is better than a dashboard for Obenan customers. A small exploratory study of screenless agent interaction found that participants liked efficiency for simple tasks but lacked confidence in complex delegation without richer feedback [E18]. That result is an analogy, not direct product evidence.

### Claim boundary

Do not claim "no dashboard" if setup, security administration, history, evidence review, approvals, exception handling, or recovery normally requires one. A more precise benefit may be "No new dashboard to manage day to day" or "You do not have to live in another dashboard," but only when routine supported jobs genuinely work through the claimed path.

### Minimum product evidence

- A mapped set of core customer jobs and which can be completed without opening a dashboard.
- Proof that onboarding, normal work, status, approval, correction, and common exceptions are supported on that path.
- Evidence and action history that remain retrievable even if no dashboard is used.
- Clear fallback for outages, complex review, accessibility needs, and administrative controls.
- Direct usability research comparing task completion, errors, confidence, recovery, and perceived burden with the relevant alternative.
- Honest disclosure of any dashboard-only or visual-review steps.

## Boundaries for preferred-assistant access

Access through a customer's preferred assistant is an interface and integration claim, not proof of agentic competence. "Preferred" also implies choice: support for one route does not establish customer preference or universal compatibility.

### Minimum product and security gates

1. **Supported-route matrix:** Current assistant, version, tenant, locale, and exact read or write workflows are documented.
2. **Identity binding:** The customer, account, organization, and target resource are bound correctly on every request.
3. **Least privilege:** Permissions are scoped, time-bounded where appropriate, non-transferable, and no greater than the authorizing human's permissions [E24-E27].
4. **No credential ambiguity:** Token audience is validated; tokens are not passed through to unrelated downstream systems; secure storage and revocation are proven [E25, E27].
5. **Tool transparency:** Available tools and material invocations are visible; sensitive actions have risk-appropriate confirmation and denial paths [E26].
6. **Input and output safety:** Inputs are validated, untrusted content cannot silently expand authority, outputs are checked, and errors are explicit.
7. **Receipts and read-back:** The customer can distinguish requested, approved, attempted, delivered, verified, partial, and failed states.
8. **Recovery:** Timeouts, retries, idempotency, rollback, outage fallback, and escalation have been tested.
9. **Privacy:** Data access, consent, retention, disclosure, and cross-tenant isolation are documented and tested.
10. **Compatibility evidence:** Positive, negative, authorization, replay, revocation, accessibility, locale, and provider-change tests pass end to end.

Until these gates pass, safe internal wording is "proposed access route" or "supported in a limited workflow," not "use Obenan in your preferred assistant."

## Product-evidence gates synthesis must not cross

1. **Capability gate:** Current supported jobs, objects, sources, systems, locales, and exclusions are documented.
2. **Authority gate:** Every action class has an owner, authorization rule, privilege boundary, expiry or revocation path, and risk tier.
3. **State gate:** The product distinguishes observed, inferred, recommended, prepared, approved, delivered, verified, partial, and failed states.
4. **Execution gate:** Real provider or system receipts prove that approved actions reached the exact target.
5. **Verification gate:** Authoritative read-back or downstream postconditions prove the intended result without implying future permanence.
6. **Control gate:** Correction, dismissal, approval, override, stop, and rollback work at meaningful times, and their effectiveness has been tested.
7. **Reliability gate:** Coverage, error rates, false positives and negatives, timeouts, retries, exceptions, and monitoring health are measured over an appropriate period.
8. **Security and privacy gate:** Identity, tenant isolation, least privilege, token handling, consent, retention, audit, and incident recovery are independently reviewed.
9. **Interface gate:** Each claimed assistant route and dashboard-free workflow passes end-to-end positive, negative, compatibility, accessibility, and recovery tests.
10. **Customer-evidence gate:** Direct research shows the claimed route reduces effort or friction without reducing comprehension, control, error detection, or confidence.

No synthesis may turn a proposal, prototype, draft, test, receipt, or isolated successful run into a production-wide capability claim. No evidence in this design workspace supports guarantees of AI discovery, recommendation, ranking, indexing, revenue, bookings, or autonomous execution in external systems.

## Final recommendations

1. Make **bounded delegation with proof** the research model: scope, authority, visibility, meaningful control, verification, accountability and recovery, and interface fit.
2. Replace universal autonomy language with the bounded direction "handles the supported ongoing work on your behalf," only after the supported scope is current and provable.
3. Require the claim ladder on every customer-facing workflow so prepared, approved, delivered, and verified can never collapse into one state.
4. Gate the verbs monitors, prepares, decides, acts, fixes, handles, and delivers against the minimum-proof table before they enter copy.
5. Use risk-tiered control: reversible routine actions may operate within explicit authorization, while high-impact or hard-to-reverse actions require meaningful approval.
6. Treat explanations as context, not proof. Pair them with provenance, state, receipts, read-back, failure visibility, and tested correction.
7. Keep correction, denial, stop, revocation, rollback, and escalation visible enough to be usable, then measure whether oversight works rather than counting prompts.
8. Present reduced dashboard dependence and preferred-assistant access only as conditional, route-specific benefits backed by current security, compatibility, workflow, and user evidence.
9. Remove fear, shame, anthropomorphic absolutes, "stay ahead," universal coverage, and guaranteed outcomes; require Seven's approval for the exact final wording.
