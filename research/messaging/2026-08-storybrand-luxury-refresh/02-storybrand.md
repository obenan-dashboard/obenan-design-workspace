## Executive verdict

The February dataset gets the durable StoryBrand spine substantially right: the customer is the hero, the brand is the guide, a clear problem creates relevance, empathy and authority establish guide posture, a simple plan reduces uncertainty, a clear call to action identifies the next step, and success should be easier to see than failure.

It becomes unreliable when it turns those principles into a deterministic communication operating system. StoryBrand's current official material does not support the February document's fixed authority-to-warmth ratios, mandatory pairing and repetition counts for calls to action, one-to-three failure-to-success ratio, universal prospect psychologies, precise neuroscience claims, or promise of predictable conversion. Its Obenan examples also invent customer biographies, amplify fear and shame, anchor language to named platforms, and make unsupported claims about autonomy, discovery, recommendation, revenue, visibility, and complete execution.

The current StoryBrand homepage is useful as a structural reference, not a copy source. It creates fast comprehension through one dominant idea, broad recognition of a messaging difficulty, immediate reassurance, a visible next action, and progressively disclosed choices. Obenan should transfer that hierarchy while using its own evidence: recognize concern about keeping up with AI change, offer bounded relief through ongoing work Obenan can actually perform, establish guide credibility with proof, and avoid diagnosing a detailed prospect life that research has not established. StoryBrand should be used as a diagnostic lens, not as evidence that a claim is true or a message will convert.

## A. What the current official framework actually says

The seven-part framework remains current. StoryBrand's current [learn-the-framework page](https://storybrand.com/learn-the-framework/) still describes a seven-part framework, and its current [Building a StoryBrand 2.0 page](https://storybrand.com/building-a-storybrand-book-new/) calls it a seven-part story formula. The fully revised book was released on 2025-01-07, according to the [publisher announcement](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20241121631078/en/Bestselling-Author-Donald-Miller-to-Release-Building-a-StoryBrand-2.0-With-HarperCollins-Leadership-on-January-7-2025).

The most detailed first-party public definitions are in the [StoryBrand Online Marketing Course Workbook](https://storybrand.com/downloads/StoryBrand-Online-Marketing-Course-Workbook.pdf). The file is currently accessible, but its embedded creation date is 2018-10-29 and its server last-modified date is 2023-01-28. It is therefore authoritative for the framework's documented mechanics, but not evidence that every website tactic in it is newly published in 2026.

| Element | Official framework meaning | February assessment | Bounded Obenan transfer |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Customer or character | The customer is the hero. Start by identifying a shared desire and, where useful, an aspirational identity. | Accurate at the principle level. The rule that every individual communication must literally start and end with the customer is mechanical. | Define one evidence-backed desire per page. Do not invent team structure, skill, workload, tools, maturity, or behavior. |
| Problem | State the conflict that makes the offer relevant. StoryBrand distinguishes external, internal, and philosophical problems. The workbook treats a villain as an optional bonus section. | The three levels are accurate. Calling the internal problem the universal purchase driver and requiring it to be explicit in every communication overstates the source. | The founder-directed felt concern is keeping up with AI change. | Guide | A guide helps the hero succeed by expressing empathy and demonstrating authority or competency. The guide serves the customer's story rather than becoming its protagonist. | Accurate until the dataset invents a 65/35 authority-to-approachability ratio. No reviewed official source supplies that ratio. | Use one brief recognition statement and then verifiable proof, boundaries, or experience. Obenan's activity is important, but the customer's relief and agency remain the point. |
| Plan | A simple plan lowers perceived risk and confusion. The workbook recommends three or four clear steps and also discusses process plans, value agreements, and simple paradigm shifts. | Mostly accurate, but "always use three steps" is too rigid for every message or page. | Show only the steps needed to make the next action feel safe. | Call to action | Calls to action should be clear. The workbook distinguishes direct calls to action from transitional calls to action and encourages consistent wording. | The types are accurate. Add a secondary action only when it serves a distinct visitor need without weakening the primary path. |
| Failure | Truthfully define what may be lost if the customer does not act. The workbook explicitly warns that too much fear causes disengagement and treats failure as a small amount of the message. | The light-touch principle is accurate. The fixed one-to-three ratio and fear-heavy Obenan examples are not. | Failure is optional in the hero. If used later, state only a verified consequence without shame, competitive threat, inevitability, or urgency theatre. |
| Success | Cast a positive, concrete vision of what life can look and feel like after the problem is resolved. Use both words and images where appropriate. | Accurate as direction, but many February examples convert a vision into guaranteed discovery, growth, bookings, visibility, or autonomous completion. | Lead with credible relief, continuity, clarity, or regained attention. Pair emotional relief with product outcomes Obenan can prove, not downstream guarantees it cannot control. |

### Guide posture without making Obenan the hero

**A. Observed:** The framework makes the guide a strong character whose purpose is to help the hero succeed. It does not require the guide to disappear. Empathy and competency are both visible.

**B. February claim:** The dataset correctly says the brand is not the protagonist, but its Obenan sound bites repeatedly make a tireless, autonomous AI system the central character.

**C. Interpretation:** "Customer as hero" is a relationship rule, not a ban on describing what Obenan does. Obenan may be the active subject of a sentence when the sentence resolves customer uncertainty and remains within product boundaries. The error is making product cleverness, scale, or autonomy the emotional destination.

**D. Recommendation:** Use this sequence: recognize the customer's concern, describe the bounded ongoing work Obenan handles, show proof and limits, then return to the customer's relief, control, or next step.

## A. How the recent StoryBrand homepage creates clarity and emotional relevance

The [live StoryBrand homepage](https://storybrand.com/) retrieved on 2026-08-19 showed a dated training for 2026-08-20, making it current to the cutoff. Its opening hierarchy does five useful things:

1. The headline combines a branded action with one result: "clarify your message so customers engage."
2. The supporting text names a broad difficulty without a detailed biography, normalizes it, and quickly moves to reassurance.
3. A dominant action follows immediately, with a quieter diagnostic quiz for visitors who do not yet know which path fits.
4. The next section repeats the same clarity theme before presenting three ways to buy, each differentiated by format, duration, investment, and fit.
5. Authority appears through named expertise, specific engagement structures, and deliverables rather than through a long company origin story in the hero.

### What creates clarity

- One repeated concept organizes the page: message clarity.
- Headline, support, and action each have one job.
- Labels such as duration, investment, and best fit reduce comparison effort.
- The page moves from a single promise to progressively more detailed choices.
- The page uses a consistent verb for the desired action instead of rotating through unrelated calls to action.

### What creates emotional relevance

- The support copy acknowledges that the task can feel difficult.
- It reduces isolation before introducing the service.
- It offers help immediately instead of lecturing the visitor about marketing theory.
- The recognition is broad enough that it does not need to assert the visitor's company size, team, tools, sophistication, or daily behavior.

### What should not transfer mechanically

- StoryBrand can use its own name as a verb because the page is selling a known proprietary method. Obenan should not lead with an unfamiliar category label that customers must decode.
- StoryBrand's page assumes the visitor has a messaging problem. Obenan's recognition must stay within the founder-directed concern until customer research supports more detail.
- The page makes growth and sales claims that are first-party marketing claims, not independent causal evidence.
- The page presents three commercial options and a quiz. This directly contradicts the February dataset's claim that premium service should prescribe one option and avoid choices.
- The observed hierarchy is transferable. Its phrasing, branded terms, conversion claims, prices, scarcity cues, and offer architecture are not.

## B. What the February dataset gets right

1. **Customer and guide roles.** The customer should be invited into a story about their desired outcome, with the brand supplying help and competency.
2. **Problem before mechanism.** Naming a relevant problem is more useful than leading with internal product architecture.
3. **Internal tension matters.** A felt concern can make an external product function meaningful, provided that concern is observed rather than invented.
4. **Clarity and inside-language control.** Plain language, one dominant idea, and removal of unexplained jargon are consistent with official StoryBrand guidance.
5. **A plan can reduce risk.** A short process can make the next step easier to understand.
6. **Calls to action should be explicit.** Visitors should not need to infer what happens next.
7. **Failure should be restrained.** The dataset's "salt" metaphor comes from StoryBrand, although the dataset violates it in many examples.
8. **Success deserves more space.** A credible positive future should be easier to see than the feared outcome.
9. **The founder story belongs in guide posture.** It may establish empathy and authority when verified, but should not displace the customer.
10. **Feature-to-outcome translation is useful.** The customer needs to know why a capability matters, while proof and mechanism must remain when they establish credibility or boundaries.

## C and D. Obenan implications as practical rules

1. **Start with one shared desire.** Use the founder-directed concern about keeping up with AI change as the current strategic direction, but do not represent it as customer research until voice-of-customer evidence confirms it.
2. **Create recognition without biography.** Name the concern at a level that does not assume overload, incompetence, team structure, hiring intent, current tools, or operating behavior.
3. **Make the support line carry product truth.** After the felt problem or relief, state who Obenan serves and the bounded ongoing work it handles. Do not hide a necessary limit behind emotional language.
4. **Show guide posture through recognition plus proof.** One warm acknowledgment is enough. Follow it with verified capability, evidence, customer proof, or an explicit boundary.
5. **Use a plan to reduce risk, not decorate the page.** Show only the steps the customer needs to understand connection, control, approval, operation, and verification.
6. **Give one action visual priority.** The primary CTA must describe a real next step. A secondary CTA is justified only for a distinct readiness level or information need.
7. **Treat failure as optional and factual.** Do not use competitive threats, future inevitability, shame, lost-customer arithmetic, or unsupported urgency.
8. **Describe success inside Obenan's control.** Relief, continuity, clearer control, completed supported work, and verifiable state may be credible. Discovery, recommendation, ranking, revenue, and bookings remain downstream possibilities.
9. **Retain enough mechanism to earn trust.** Translating a feature into a benefit does not justify deleting the evidence, approval model, failure state, or boundary that makes the benefit believable.
10. **Test recognition and comprehension separately.** A message can be clear but emotionally wrong, or emotionally resonant but unclear about the offer. Both require evidence before canonization.

### Headline and support-copy hierarchy

This is a structural rule set, not final approved copy:

1. **Optional factual eyebrow:** audience or context only when it improves orientation.
2. **Headline:** one felt concern or one credible relief. No category machinery, platform names, competitive claim, or compound promise.
3. **Support sentence:** who Obenan is for, what bounded ongoing job it handles, and the practical relief. Use a second sentence only if a material control or limitation must be visible immediately.
4. **Primary CTA:** the exact real next step.
5. **Quiet secondary action:** only if it answers a different readiness need.
6. **Immediate proof:** evidence, coverage, process, or verified customer language that substantiates the promise.
7. **Plan:** the minimum crossable steps, including customer control where relevant.

## Final recommendations

1. Retain SB7 as a diagnostic checklist, not as a definitive operating system or causal guarantee.
2. Preserve the customer-as-hero and Obenan-as-guide relationship, with visible Obenan activity always returning to customer relief and agency.
3. Build the hero around one evidence-backed concern or desired relief, followed by a bounded product explanation and one real next step.
4. Replace invented prospect detail with broad, recognizable language until customer research supports greater specificity.
5. Establish guide authority through proof, capability boundaries, and honest states, not superlatives, scale theatre, or autonomy language.
6. Use the shortest plan that makes control, approval, operation, and verification clear.
7. Make stakes optional, truthful, and light. Prohibit shame, competitive fear, and unsupported urgency.
8. Describe success through relief and outcomes Obenan can verify. Keep discovery, recommendation, ranking, revenue, and bookings outside the promise.
9. Remove the February dataset's fixed ratios, CTA counts, deterministic neuroscience, universal word substitutions, and unsourced luxury rules from reusable guidance.
10. Require customer-language validation, product-claim review, comprehension testing, and Seven's approval before any research finding becomes messaging canon.

## Limitations

- Full current paid StoryBrand training, the full revised 2.0 book, and gated Messaging Canvas materials were not available for direct inspection.
- Several detailed public StoryBrand PDFs are currently hosted but were created between 2015 and 2018. They were used for framework mechanics and explicitly labeled as older artifacts.
- No controlled study testing StoryBrand itself was identified in the reviewed source set. General narrative and fluency research cannot establish Obenan conversion effects.
- This specialist scope did not include an Obenan customer-language corpus, product capability audit, legal claim review, analytics, or live conversion experiment.
- The live homepage is a single observed state on 2026-08-19 and may change.
